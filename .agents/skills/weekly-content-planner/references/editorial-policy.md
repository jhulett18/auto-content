# Locked-In Supply editorial policy

## Objective and audience

Optimize the calendar for sustainable audience growth and trust among Pokemon TCG collectors. Every post should give the reader a concrete reason to save, share, comment, or return. Avoid turning the feed into a stream of urgent alerts or direct sales pitches.

## Editorial pillars

- `education`: durable collector knowledge and practical explainers.
- `market-literacy`: methods for interpreting prices, supply, condition, and demand without price predictions or financial advice.
- `drop-readiness`: calm, actionable preparation for releases and restocks without unsupported urgency.
- `community`: identity, collecting stories, taste, and prompts that encourage substantive participation.

Aim for one post from each pillar every week. If the bank cannot support that mix, use at most two posts from one pillar and explain the substitution.

## Selection order

Rank eligible ideas by:

1. Evidence quality and the ability to distinguish verified fact from opinion.
2. Audience usefulness and a clear save/share/comment reason.
3. Freshness, including a real deadline for timely ideas.
4. Variety relative to the preceding eight weekly plans.
5. Fit for a concise, visually coherent three-slide carousel.
6. Topic-bank priority.

Do not repeat the same premise within eight weeks unless the user requests an update or there is materially new information. Explain any deliberate repeat.

## Research and factuality

- Browse before using current claims. Prefer official Pokemon, retailer, event, manufacturer, government, or platform sources. Use reputable specialist sources only when no primary source covers the claim.
- Record sources beside the claims they support and include the date checked.
- Separate sourced facts, reasonable interpretation, and audience prompts. Never turn speculation into a headline.
- Exclude an idea when its central claim cannot be verified. Keep it in the bank with a note explaining what evidence is missing.
- Avoid investment promises, guaranteed availability, fabricated scarcity, or instructions that bypass retailer rules.

## Brief standard

Each post must include:

- The audience promise and why it belongs on that day.
- A three-slide narrative: hook, useful explanation, and takeaway or discussion prompt.
- Exact headline and body copy short enough for deterministic rendering.
- A caption and one CTA.
- Claim-level source links for factual statements.
- A primary reference recommendation and the transferable traits to use.

## Reference strategy

Use one primary reference across all three slides to preserve hierarchy, palette, typography, pacing, and composition. Derive only abstract traits. Never reproduce source wording, logos, creator identity, characters, card art, packaging, fake interfaces, or promotional calls to action.

Optionally name up to two secondary references, each with one narrow role such as `palette only`, `pacing only`, or `composition only`. Secondary references are notes, not renderer inputs. If multiple references disagree, the primary reference wins.

Prefer references whose structure fits the brief. Do not choose solely because their subject matches the topic. Avoid references that depend on branded packaging or recognizable artwork for their visual impact.
